OpenCane Hardware Brief

The phone does the seeing, thinking, and talking. The kit adds two small things to a normal white cane: a phone mount and a haptic handle. Weather, maps, and object ID are software. Everything below is sized for a 24-hour build and a cheap product.

The modules

Two required, two optional off-the-shelf, three cut. Each clips on or pairs; none needs the others to be useful.

Required · print

Phone mount

  • PETG shaft clamp + cradle that grips the phone's long edges only. Keep the camera and LiDAR corner clear.
  • Fixed angle on one M5 bolt with serrated faces, not a free ball joint. Ball joints slip under sweep vibration and break the Point-to-Identify calibration.
  • Place 15–25 cm below the grip. A 233 g phone lower on the shaft means more wrist torque every sweep.
  • Printed lip over the camera block as a rain hood.
  • Plan B: buy a bike phone mount head, print only the shaft adapter.
  • Dev stick shaft: 28.75 mm (1.132 in). Real white canes are thinner, so give the clamp swappable shims.
Required · print + solder

Haptic handle

  • XIAO ESP32C3: $4.99, 21 × 17.5 mm, BLE 5.0, built-in LiPo charging.
  • 3 coin motors, 10 × 3 mm, 3 V: Left, Right, Thumb.
  • 2 tactile buttons under a printed rocker cap = the "dual-mode rocker".
  • 1S LiPo, 300–500 mAh, plus a slide power switch.
  • Guarded SOS button on the end cap, away from the rocker.
  • Dumb by design: only debounces buttons and plays buzz patterns. All logic lives on the phone.
Demo · team-owned

AirPods Pro

  • Run in Transparency mode so traffic stays audible. Pro and Max models only.
  • Bonus: head tracking (CMHeadphoneMotionManager) lets the app place speech in space, so "door" sounds from the right.
  • Production: open-ear or bone conduction. Anything in the ear canal is the first objection blind travelers will raise.
Optional · buy

Clip-on light

  • Bike light on the shaft. Drivers see the user at night; camera detection improves.
  • LiDAR is its own light source, so depth works in the dark regardless.
Cut

IMU, tip sensor, weather sensors

  • IMU: phone already has one on the same rigid shaft.
  • Tip ToF/ultrasonic: LiDAR + the cane itself.
  • Temp/humidity/rain sensor: WeatherKit knows the weather at the user's GPS position.

Why the DRV2605L haptic driver ($7.95) is not in v1: nicer waveforms, but its I²C address is fixed, so three motors need a multiplexer. Worth it for a product, not for 24 hours.

Intensity 0–7. Pattern: 0 single 80 ms, 1 double, 2 on until next command, 3 ramp. 0x00 stops all. Sent as write-without-response.

Handle → phone: one byte per event

High nibble = button (1 up, 2 down, 3 SOS). Low nibble = gesture (1 click, 2 double, 3 hold start, 4 hold end). Sent as notify. Battery level rides on the standard Battery Service (0x180F).

  • Latency. iOS allows a 15 ms minimum connection interval. At normal walking speed (~1.3 m/s) 100 ms of total delay is about 13 cm of travel. BLE is not the bottleneck; the vision pipeline is.
  • Link-lost rule. If BLE drops, the handle plays three long thumb buzzes on its own. The user must always know when guidance is off.
  • Background. The app needs the bluetooth-central background mode so buzzes keep coming with the screen locked.

Emergency button

A lifeline the user pulls. Never one the AI pulls for them.

  1. Hold the SOS button 2 s. It sits recessed under a printed guard ring, so a bump or a pocket can't fire it.
  2. Countdown. Thumb motor ramps up; phone says "Calling for help in 5. Press any button to cancel."
  3. Call goes out. Phone hits the backend; Twilio calls the chosen contact with a spoken message and texts a live map link.
  4. Confirmation. Slow steady thumb pulse while help is being reached; one long buzz when someone picks up.

Pushback

  • No AI-triggered 911. Camera "threat" detection misfires, and a misfire sends police toward a bystander. Judges will ask. The AI may say "Someone is approaching fast. Hold SOS to call for help." The user decides.
  • Camera blindness is not an emergency. When the lens is covered or it's too dark, the right answer is a distinct buzz and "I can't see. Slow down."
  • Never dial 911 to test. On stage, say production routes to 911 through a dispatch partner. The demo calls a teammate.
  • Single point of failure. The button needs phone + BLE. If the link drops, the handle plays link-lost and the user falls back to side-button SOS or the Watch. Teach both in onboarding.
  • Stretch: drop detection. Phone sees an impact, then no motion for ~20 s: "Are you OK?" then countdown to the contact. A dropped cane isn't a fallen user, so it always asks first.

Wiring

Three identical motor channels and three buttons. No I²C needed now the IMU is gone.

XIAO pinGPIOGoes to
D131 kΩ → 2N2222 base, Left motor
D241 kΩ → 2N2222 base, Right motor
D351 kΩ → 2N2222 base, Thumb motor
D46Up button → GND (INPUT_PULLUP)
D57Down button → GND (INPUT_PULLUP)
D621SOS button → GND (INPUT_PULLUP). UART TX pin; fine while serial runs over USB (enable USB CDC On Boot).
BAT+ / BAT−—LiPo via slide switch (pads on board underside)
  • Each motor: motor + to battery +, motor − to transistor collector, emitter to GND. 1N4148 across the motor, stripe toward battery +.
  • Motors are rated 3 V; a full LiPo is 4.2 V. Cap PWM duty around 70% in firmware.
  • Keep buttons off D0, D8, D9 (GPIO 2, 8, 9). Those are boot strapping pins; a held button at power-on can drop the board into bootloader mode.
  • Measure real current draw with a USB meter before picking the final battery size.

Print notes

For Sagar. Measure first, then print the mount before the handle.

  • Measured: dev stick shaft is 28.75 mm. Still decide whether the handle slides over it (bore ~29.1 mm, outer diameter ~38 mm) or replaces the top section.
  • SOS guard: raised ring ~3 mm proud of the button top on the end cap or underside. Round and ringed, so it never feels like the rocker.
  • Handle: split clamshell, ~160 mm long, PETG, 4 walls, 25–30% gyroid, M3 heat-set inserts. Add +0.3 mm clearance on v1.
  • Motor pockets: Left on the left flank under the fingers, Right on the right flank, Thumb next to the rocker. Wrap each motor in TPU or foam tape and keep them as far apart as the grip allows. In a rigid shell all three buzzes blend together.
  • Backup encoding: if L/R still blur, the phone can also send Left = 1 pulse, Right = 2 pulses. Direction survives even when location doesn't.
  • Mount clamp: PETG, solid around the bolt holes. Orient so clamping force squeezes layers together instead of peeling them apart. Rubber or TPU shim inside for grip.

Test before the demo

  • Blindfold L/R test. 20 random buzzes per teammate. Aim for 18+ correct. If not, add isolation or switch to pulse-count encoding.
  • Phone heat and battery. Run LiDAR + detector for 30 minutes, log battery drop. Unknown until measured. Bring a MagSafe battery.
  • Mount slip. Sweep for 10 minutes, check the angle hasn't moved.
  • Sweep blur. Watch detections during full-speed sweeps; if poor, run detection only near sweep endpoints.
  • Overhead reach. Walk under a low sign or branch at demo mount angle. Does the frame see it?
  • Link loss. Kill the app mid-walk. Handle must play the three-buzz warning.
  • SOS dry run. Countdown, cancel, then a real call to a teammate's verified number. Never 911.
  • Route. Walk ISR → CIF once with the full kit before judging.
